What is not proven

Stating the ceiling is part of the result. Everything below is a known limit of the bench or of the method, not an oversight.

  • Telemetry delivery. The uCentral agent is disabled by design, so the suite can prove the collector runs and the metrics were accepted, but not that anything reached a controller.
  • Anything needing a second station. Strict forwarding, client isolation, steering and quality-threshold all need two clients. The Pi's onboard radio is deliberately disabled — using it cost a bricked boot. A USB adapter is the route, not the onboard chip.
  • Roaming between APs. One AP on the bench means 802.11r/k/v are checked as advertised capability, not as a completed roam.
  • 6 GHz and Wi-Fi 7. Permanently skipped until an AP with a 6 GHz radio is on the bench. The BE200 client side is ready.
  • Tunnels and mesh. They need a peer AP or tunnel endpoint.
  • Rate-limit precision. The check catches a limit that did not take effect. It deliberately does not police shaper accuracy — the 2.5× ceiling is not a claim about how well the AP shapes.
  • IPv6. Out of scope; the lab has no IPv6, so those cases were failing for an environmental reason and saying nothing about the AP.

The rule that has cost the most time

A bench fault reads exactly like an AP defect. Three separate sweeps were destroyed by the bench rather than the AP — a station that fell over, hundreds of station SSH errors, and a RADIUS container that had exited without restarting. Every one produced a plausible-looking pile of AP failures. That is why preflight blocks a run, and why an overridden preflight is recorded against the run for ever.
